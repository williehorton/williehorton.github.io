01. Sales operating system, nine layers, Feb to Oct 2026

A sales operating system for an environmental services company, from a Gmail inbox to two production agents

A 16-year-old mold remediation and industrial cleaning company had no sales team, no CRM, and no written sales process. I was its first sales hire, with a brief to build and lead the team.

3 daysestimate turnaround, from a week or more

Nine layers built over nine months: lead intake and CRM, pipeline rules, field scope capture, pricing and contracts, a communications canon, outbound and public bids, the team operating system, a knowledge vault, and scheduled agents. Three reps were hired and onboarded onto it. Every decision is dated and versioned; nothing sends, signs, or prices without a person.

Role
First sales hire; designed and built every layer
In use
Feb to Oct 2026
Scale
53 inbound leads a month; 3 reps
Decisions logged
60+ CRM rulings; 10 contradictions ruled in one dated session

Lines the AI cannot cross

  • No send, sign, submit, or price. Agents produce drafts, documents, and rows; a person reviews each one.
  • No invented company facts. Bid forms are legal documents; facts come only from rows a person marked verified.
  • No client data in any AI tool, and none on the personal agent host.
What went wrong, handover

What went wrong

  • The first comp plan had a gross-profit gate that could not be enforced because 65% of jobs had no job type and no job cost was captured. It became a revenue ladder with a monthly ramp, and job-type hygiene joined the Friday sweep.
  • The first operating plan had no ops capacity ceiling and no pricing authority policy, so sales could outsell the crew. Version 3 added both.
  • Six months of documents written without a register produced ten contradictions. I ruled on all ten in one dated session and commissioned a cross-system document audit so it would not happen again.
  • Lead-to-scope conversion was never instrumented. Scope-to-sign (22%) and proposal close rate (about 60%) have different denominators and need reconciling before either goes on a slide.

Handover

  • The sales manager role I designed was not adopted; the CEO took over the team in October and the system runs without me in that seat. That was the point of writing it all down.

03. Follow-up priority logic, one design, three implementations

Follow-up priority logic that computes who to call first

One salesperson with about forty open conversations and no system for follow-up order. Leads went cold because they arrived on busy days and nothing tracked who had waited longest.

1 hourfirst-contact standard, same business day

Pipeline stage, last inbound, last outbound, and the first-response standard feed one formula that sorts the list each morning. Written as a specification first, then built as an Airtable CRM, carried into a Lovable and Supabase dashboard, and now driving the company's lead system through a Calendly webhook. The tools changed; the rules did not. There is no AI in it: a rule gives the same answer every time.

Why there is no AI in it

05. Estimate drafter that cannot invent a line item

An estimate drafter that cannot invent a line item

As account manager and estimator for a Portland general contractor I wrote thirty to forty proposals a month, most of each afternoon spent looking up catalog items and doing arithmetic by hand.

30 to 40proposals a month, drafted for review instead of built by hand

A prompt turns scope notes and measurements into a line-item proposal using only the company's approved Zoho catalog, with project management and mobilization as fixed percentages. If the work needs an item not in the catalog, the draft says so and stops. Every line prints its dimensions, its source, and its math.

Role
Estimator; built it, used it
Catalog
Exact item IDs, names, rates only
Fixed math
PM 3.123%, mobilization 3.43%
Output
Confirmed section by section, then JSON

What the prompt enforces

  1. Items come only from the approved catalog export, by exact ID, name, and rate. No synonyms, no near matches.
  2. Labor and material quantities are paired per line; a material line without its labor line is an error.
  3. Project management is 3.123% of materials, sub labor, sub materials, and finish materials. Mobilization is 3.43% of the labor subtotal. Both fixed.
  4. Permits, engineering, and plans appear only when the scope requests them.
  5. Each section is drafted, then confirmed by the estimator, before the next section is drafted.
  6. Every line prints what is happening and why, the dimensions and measurements, where each number came from, and the arithmetic.
  7. Final output is customer-keyed JSON for the estimating system, produced only after approval.

The rule set from the prompt, as specified in September 2025.

Lines the AI cannot cross

  • Nothing outside the catalog, ever.
  • No permits, engineering, or plans unless asked.
  • No hidden calculations. "Show the math" is a hard rule.
What I learned, handover

What I learned

  • The model is good at matching work to catalog items and writing descriptions, and unreliable at arithmetic across a long proposal. Requiring it to show the math made the output checkable. I apply the same rule in every system I build.

Handover

  • The prompt is one document with its rules at the top. Anyone with the catalog export can run it.

06. QA-gated document agent, 124 of 124 delivered clean

An agent that standardizes pharmacy prior-authorization criteria for a regional health plan

The plan's pharmacy team needed one formatted criteria document per drug, assembled from a template, a published criteria PDF, and a quantity-limit list. Formatting had to match the template exactly and wording had to be verbatim, because these documents inform coverage decisions.

124documents in 6 batches, Aug to Oct 2026; every one passed 8 automated checks

The agent edits a copy of the approved template, extracts criteria word for word, and runs eight checks after every build, from byte-level template preservation to a cross-check of each approval duration against its source sentence. A failed check blocks the document. Published criteria only; no patient data; separated from every other client's files.

Role
Sole builder, contract engagement
Delivered
124 documents, 6 batches
QA
8 automated checks; 124 of 124 pass
Data
Published criteria, no patient data

The eight checks, from the builder's QA script

  1. check_template_preservation: the output's archive members match the template byte for byte except the parts the builder is allowed to touch.
  2. check_container_integrity: the .docx container is structurally valid after surgery.
  3. check_sectpr: section properties, page size, and margins identical to the template.
  4. check_table_and_title: the criteria table matches the template except the two changed cells; title formatting untouched.
  5. check_numbering_refs: list numbering is additive only; original definitions untouched, each added list a faithful clone.
  6. check_content: no placeholder text, no invented identifiers, every criteria line present verbatim, every approval duration cross-checked against its own source sentence.
  7. check_footer: Revised and Effective dates landed where the template expects them, or were deliberately blanked.
  8. check_python_docx: the document reopens cleanly in a second library with the fields that must be blank confirmed blank.

Any failed check blocks the document. The script gates on exit codes, not log text, so a crash cannot be mistaken for a pass.

Lines the AI cannot cross

  • No clinical inference: no invented identifiers, no assumed approval duration, no paraphrased criteria.
  • No sending. Output lands in a review folder.
  • No contact with any other client's files or agents.
What broke, handover

What broke

  • A QA check had its own bug. The approval-duration test hardcoded the phrase "for 12 months," so it only ever confirmed that 12-month pathways were 12-month pathways. A drug with a 24-month pathway failed it, and the failure was in the check, not the document. I rewrote it to compare against whatever duration the source declares, which also catches the reverse error the old version could never see: a source stating a duration the document omits.
  • Rules that lived only in a working session faded between sessions, and the agent drifted back to default behavior. Every rule now lives in the definition the agent reads on every run, with a backup saved before each policy change.

What maturity looks like

  • The sixth batch of 33 drugs needed no new QA rules; existing checks handled everything it raised. Where a source document was malformed (a date with three digits in the day field), the agent transcribed it as written and flagged it rather than guessing a correction.

Handover

  • A README documents the writer strategy and every QA check. Adding a drug means adding one data file. Shared code never changes.

Client documents are not shown. The QA checks above are the public record of how each one was verified.

How I build

  1. Draft first; a person approves.The system prepares the work; a person signs off. Autonomy is extended one task type at a time, based on track record. When a message type has run clean for months and a miss is cheap, the review becomes a sample instead of a gate.
  2. Rules for the math, AI for the text.Priority, pricing, deadlines, and permissions are formulas and code that can be audited. The model reads, classifies, and writes; it does not do arithmetic.
  3. Small first version, then expand from real use.Build the smallest version that helps, keep a raw copy of every input, retire a tool when a better one removes a limit, and add features only where use shows they are needed.

The build is about a third of the work. The rest is writing the procedure, training the people who will use it, and checking at thirty days whether they are. Systems run on the organization's accounts, with documentation for whoever comes next.
